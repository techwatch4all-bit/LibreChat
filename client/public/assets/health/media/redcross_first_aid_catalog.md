## Section 1: Core Life-Support & Resuscitation (Even a Child Can Follow)

### 1. The Recovery Position (Unresponsive but Breathing Normally)
*When someone is knocked out, faints, or is asleep and won't wake up, but their chest is rising and falling normally.*

```
                 Tilt Head Gently Back (Airway Open)
                           \   o
                            \ / \_  <-- Hand under cheek supports head
                        _____(_____)____
                       /               \
        Arm 90° Angle  |   CHEST FREE  |   Top Leg Bent 90° at Knee & Hip
        ==============/                 \==============================
```

* **Step 1 (The Hand Check):** Kneel next to them. Take their arm nearest you and place it out at a right angle (like saying "hello!").
* **Step 2 (The Pillow Hand):** Take their other hand across their chest and put the back of their hand against their opposite cheek (this becomes a soft pillow for their head).
* **Step 3 (The Kickstand Leg):** Bend their far leg at the knee so their foot is flat on the floor.
* **Step 4 (The Gentle Roll):** Pull the bent knee towards you. The person rolls onto their side smoothly.
* **Step 5 (Keep the Breathing Tube Open):** Tilt their chin back slightly so their mouth faces the floor. If they vomit, it will drool out safely instead of choking them.

### 2. Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR) & Defibrillation
![Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation](/assets/health/media/cardiopulmonary_resuscitation.jpg)

* **When to Do CPR:** The person is **unresponsive AND not breathing** (or only making occasional gasping/snoring sounds called agonal gasps).
* **Child-Friendly Song Rhythm:** Push hard and fast in the center of the chest to the beat of the song *"Stayin' Alive"* (100 to 120 beats per minute).
* **Adult CPR Protocol:**
  - Heel of one hand in the center of the chest (between nipples), interlock the fingers of the other hand on top.
  - Keep elbows locked straight and shoulders directly over your hands.
  - Push down firmly at least **2 inches (5 cm)** and let the chest bounce all the way back up after each push.
  - Ratio: **30 chest compressions $\to$ 2 gentle rescue breaths** (or continuous "Hands-Only CPR" if untrained in breaths).
* **Child & Infant CPR Variations:**
  - **Child (1–8 years):** Use 1 or 2 hands depending on child size; compress 2 inches; start with 5 initial rescue breaths.
  - **Infant (<1 year):** Use **2 fingers** on the center of the breastbone (just below nipple line); compress 1.5 inches (4 cm); seal your mouth over both infant's nose and mouth for rescue puffs.

### 3. Severe Choking & Airway Obstruction
![Choking First Aid Protocol](/assets/health/media/choking.jpg)

* **Universal Choking Sign:** Person clutches their throat with both hands, cannot speak, cry, or cough, and faces turn red/blue.
* **Child & Adult Rescue Cycle (The "5 & 5 Rule"):**
  1. **5 Sharp Back Blows:** Stand behind and slightly to the side. Support their chest with one hand, lean them forward so the stuck object falls out of their mouth. Give 5 firm blows with the heel of your hand between their shoulder blades.
  2. **5 Abdominal Thrusts (Heimlich Maneuver):** If still choked, wrap both arms around their waist. Make a fist with one hand just above their belly button. Grab your fist with the other hand and pull sharply **inward and upward**.
  3. Repeat 5 back blows and 5 belly thrusts until the object pops out.
* **Baby / Infant Choking Rescue (<1 Year):**
  - 🚨 **NEVER do abdominal thrusts on a baby** (ruptures infant liver/spleen).
  - Lay baby face down along your forearm, resting on your thigh, supporting their chin with your fingers (head lower than chest).
  - Give **5 gentle back slaps** between shoulder blades.
  - Turn baby face up along your arm, give **5 chest thrusts** using 2 fingers on the breastbone.

## Section 2: Severe Trauma, Bleeding, Fractures & Burns

### 4. Severe Bleeding Control & Hemorrhage Triage
![Bleeding Control First Aid](/assets/health/media/bleeding.jpg)

* **Direct Continuous Pressure:**
  - Take a clean cloth, towel, or sterile gauze pad and press **firmly and continuously** right over the bleeding cut.
  - 🚨 **Do not peek!** Lifting the cloth tears off the fragile initial blood clot that the body is making. If blood soaks through, add another cloth on top without removing the first.
* **Limb Elevation & Shock Position:**
  - Keep the bleeding arm or leg elevated above heart level if no bone fracture is suspected.
  - Lay the person down flat and raise their feet 12 inches to keep blood flowing to their brain and heart. Cover with a blanket to keep warm.
* **When to Use a Tourniquet:** Only for catastrophic, spurting arterial bleeding from an arm or leg that direct pressure cannot stop. Place a commercial windlass tourniquet 2-3 inches above the wound (never over a joint), twist until bleeding stops, and write the exact time on the forehead.

### 5. Broken Bones (Fractures), Sprains & Joint Dislocations
![Bone Fracture Care](/assets/health/media/bone_fracture.jpg)

* **Signs of a Broken Bone:** Sudden deformity, bone piercing skin (open/compound fracture), unnatural angle, bone grating feeling (*crepitus*), or inability to bear weight.
* **The Golden Rule:** **DO NOT MOVE THE BROKEN PART.** Keep it in the exact position found.
* **Improvised Splinting:**
  - Secure the injured limb using rolled newspapers, wooden rulers, or rolled blankets tied with cloths or neckties.
  - The splint must extend past the joint *above* and the joint *below* the fracture to stop movement.
  - Always check if the fingers or toes are warm and pink. If they turn cold, blue, or numb, the bandage is tied too tight.

---

### 6. Thermal, Chemical & Electrical Burns
![Burn Management First Aid](/assets/health/media/burn.jpg)

* **The 20-Minute Cool Running Water Rule:**
  - Immediately place the burned skin under **gentle, cool tap running water for at least 20 full minutes**.
  - Cooling within the first 3 hours halts the thermal cooking of deeper tissue layers.
* **STRICT DANGER WARNINGS:**
  - 🚨 **NEVER put ice or ice-water on a burn** (causes extreme vasoconstriction and frostbite necrosis).
  - 🚨 **NEVER apply toothpaste, butter, raw eggs, or turmeric paste to fresh burns.**
  - 🚨 **NEVER pop blisters.** Blisters are sterile biological bandages made by the body to prevent infection.
* **Protecting the Burn:** Cover loosely with clean plastic cling wrap (food wrap) or a clean, non-fluffy sterile sheet. Plastic wrap does not stick to burned skin and seals nerve endings from air pain.

## Section 3: Environmental Extremes & Critical Shocks

### 7. Heat Stroke vs Heat Exhaustion
![Heat Stroke Protocol](/assets/health/media/heat_stroke.jpg)

* **Heat Exhaustion:** Heavy sweating, pale clammy skin, nausea, weakness, dizziness, normal mental alertness.
  - *Action:* Move to shaded cool area, loosen tight clothing, sip cool water with electrolytes.
* **Heat Stroke (Life-Threatening Emergency):**
  - **Signs:** Core body temperature $> 104^\circ\text{F} (40^\circ\text{C})$, **confusion, delirium, seizures, loss of consciousness, and hot, dry or red skin (sweating mechanism fails)**.
  - *Immediate Action:* **Rapid Whole-Body Cooling.** Pour cool water over body, place ice bags or cold wet cloths under armpits, groin, and neck (where major blood vessels run), and fan vigorously until medical help arrives.

### 8. Hypothermia & Frostbite
![Hypothermia Protocol](/assets/health/media/hypothermia.jpg)

* **Signs:** Uncontrollable shivering progressing to slurred speech, clumsy fumbling fingers, apathy, confusion, and shivering *stopping* (very dangerous late stage).
* **Rewarming Protocol:**
  - Move indoors, remove wet clothing immediately.
  - Wrap in warm dry blankets (insulate underneath from the cold floor).
  - Offer warm sweet drinks (no alcohol, no caffeine).
  - 🚨 **Do not apply direct intense heat** (hot radiators, open fire, or scalding water) to frostbitten limbs; warm gradually with body heat or lukewarm water ($100^\circ\text{F} / 38^\circ\text{C}$).
